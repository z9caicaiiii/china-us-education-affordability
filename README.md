# Higher Education Costs and Affordability in China and the United States

**An exploratory comparative study using public data**  
**Author:** Yixiao Zhang  
**Date:** October 2026  
**Project type:** Independent research (not peer-reviewed or journal-published)

## Research question

How do published college costs differ between the United States and selected institutions in Shanghai, and what can publicly available income and cost measures tell us about affordability?

## Overview

This independent study compares published tuition and related economic indicators, drawing on College Board data and public information from Chinese universities and government agencies. It distinguishes between published tuition and broader costs of attendance, and examines why residency rules, income measures, and geographic coverage matter when interpreting the numbers.

The study is exploratory. Its Chinese examples focus on Shanghai, while some U.S. figures are national averages. These are **not equivalent samples**, so the paper does not claim to rank which country provides more affordable higher education.

## Key takeaway

A tuition figure alone cannot show what a student or family can afford. The scope of the price, living costs, financial assistance, and the income measures used all affect the interpretation. More closely matched student- and institution-level evidence would be needed for a fairer cross-country comparison.
